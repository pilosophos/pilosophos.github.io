After I drew my [Four of Pentacles](/art/four-of-pentacles) for the Heraldic Tarot project, another member of the Heraldry community saw it and wanted to commission their own tarot card! The requester, EtnadRehsif, previously commissioned their [coat of arms](/art/etnad-arms) from me. Thanks again for your patronage!

For this one, we had a design round where I sketched out a few different candidate designs based off of Etnad's coat of arms and on a few Knight of Pentacles cards from different decks. Tarot decks based on the famous Rider-Waite deck typically depict a young knight riding on a horse, so I sketched three knight-on-a-horse designs in different angles, where the knight was holding EtnadRehsif's shield underneath a starry sky. Then I took a break to cook some spaghetti.

It was while cooking that I had this great idea: ditch the shield, make the knight ride the manta ray on the shield instead! This worked out really well; instead of relegating EtnadRehsif's shield to a tiny bit of the card, the whole card makes use of the space-inspired design. I think it captures the wonderment of outer space that his shield represents really nicely.

Since this is the Knight of Pentacles (also known as the [Knight of Coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_of_Coins)), I have the crab in EtnadRehsif's crest holding a coin with a star struck on it, instead of the star itself. Like my own tarot card, I have EtnadRehsif's motto on the coin as well, "Strength through wisdom."

Originally, I was going to do the lettering in the same style as the "IV" in my own card. I was doing this for the "EtnadRehsif" below the manta ray, and it didn't look quite right to me. On a lark I tried writing it in cursive, and it reminded me of the names that you see painted on [US Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) aircraft in WWII sometimes (like [Old Glory](https://www.hankconrad.com/more-b-25-wwii-nose-art-a-7-image-story/) or [Executive Sweet](https://www.hankconrad.com/wwii-nose-art-b-25-executive-sweet/)). Since EtnadRehsif was *in* the US Army, I tried out an army-inspired rank and suit indicator as well: the text is based on serial numbers painted on certain military vehicles, and the suit icon is based on one of the [US Army logos](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8d/Logo_of_the_United_States_Army.svg). Conveniently, the US Army logo is also a star, like the tarot pentacle.

Altogether, this card is probably more fitting for EtnadRehsif than my own card is for myself!
